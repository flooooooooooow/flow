## Derived fact 1 — In a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of squares on the legs

**Coordinate.** the Euclidean plane · right triangle · the Pythagorean relation holds · **Derived fact**

*Source: euclid — Elements, Book I, Proposition 47*

> **Goal.** In a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of squares on the legs
>
> $$c^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2}$$

**Figure.**

![pythagoras.proof.svg](pythagoras.proof.svg)

| | **Proof** | | **Math** |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|
| ① | We prove that the Pythagorean relation holds for right triangles in the Euclidean plane. |  |  |
| ② | the square on the hypotenuse decomposes into the two squares on the legs, so c times c equals a times a plus b times b. Hence proven. | ② | $c^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2}$ |

`the Euclidean plane · right triangle · the Pythagorean relation holds`
