*Both non-right angles in a right triangle are acute.*

**Source.** euclid — Elements, Book I, Proposition 32

## Derived fact 1 — angle A is less than one right angle

**Coordinate.** the Euclidean plane · right triangle · both non-right angles are acute · **Derived fact**

*Source: euclid*

*Built on: acute angles sum to one right angle, for right triangles in on the Euclidean plane, each interior angle is less than two right, for triangles in on the Euclidean plane*

> **Goal.** angle A is less than one right angle
>
> $$\angle A < 90^\circ$$

| | **Proof** | | **Math** |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|
| ① | We prove that both non-right angles are acute for right triangles in the Euclidean plane. |  |  |
| ② | We invoke the derived fact governing right triangles in the Euclidean plane: acute angles sum to one right angle, for right triangles in on the Euclidean plane. |  |  |
| ③ | We invoke the derived fact governing triangles in the Euclidean plane: each interior angle is less than two right, for triangles in on the Euclidean plane. |  |  |
| ④ | From step 2 and step 3, this implies angle A < one right angle. Hence proven. | ④ | $\angle A < 90^\circ$ |
